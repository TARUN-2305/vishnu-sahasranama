    Sources & textual notes

    Text and edition

    The Sanskrit text is the commonly recited form of the Viṣṇu Sahasranāma from the Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva, with the traditional opening and closing verses. It was taken from Sanskrit Wikisource, “श्रीविष्णुसहस्रनामस्तोत्रम्”, a Devanāgarī conversion of the sanskritdocuments.org ITRANS edition (accessed 21 November 2026; Wikisource text is available under CC BY-SA 4.0).

    The Kannada script and the IAST lines were generated mechanically from that Devanāgarī text by a transliteration script, not retyped, so the two scripts cannot drift apart. Kannada script here records the Sanskrit; it is not a Kannada translation.

    Cross-checks

    • Every verse was compared character by character with the Mahābhārata chapter on Wikisource, “महाभारतम्-13-अनुशासनपर्व-254” (Kumbhakonam edition, with its manuscript-variant notes). Spelling-only differences (anusvāra and nasal letters) and evident scanning errors in that page are not listed below.
    • The division into names was compared with the Wikisource “श्रीविष्णुसहस्रनामावलिः” and gives exactly 1000 names in ślokas 1–107. Three groups the nāmāvalī splits are counted as one name, giving the thousand of Śaṅkara's count: śāśvataḥ sthāṇuḥ (13), maharṣiḥ kapilācāryaḥ (57) and vācaspatir ayonijaḥ (61). Repeated names (for example vedavit, vasupradaḥ, viṣṇuḥ) are counted each time.
    • A script confirms that every word in every word-by-word list occurs, in order, in its own verse.

    Editorial changes to the source

    • Main 43: the source's aside “(or विरतो)” was removed from the verse; the variant is recorded below.
    • Main 88: the source prints न्यग्रोधोऽदुम्बरो; the avagraha is a misprint (nyagrodhaḥ + udumbaraḥ join as nyagrodhodumbaraḥ, as in the Mahābhārata text), so it reads न्यग्रोधोदुम्बरो here.
    • Dhyāna 1: the line break was moved to follow the sragdharā metre (after “…pīyūṣavarṣaiḥ”). No word was changed.
    • Uttara bhāga 27: “रामरामेति” is shown as “राम रामेति”. Spacing and danda punctuation were normalised throughout.
    • Later closing verses printed after the stotra in some editions (upasaṃhāra and alternative closing verses) are not included.

    Meanings

    The English meanings and the Kannada word meanings are this edition's own wording. They follow the traditional explanations of the names, chiefly those associated with Śaṅkara's commentary, and do not reproduce any modern translation. In Study mode, notes marked Traditional interpretation report a recognised reading or a cited source; notes marked Editorial note are this edition's own explanation. Many names carry several traditional senses, and only one or two are given.

    Not yet verified: the meanings have not been reviewed by a Sanskrit scholar or a native Kannada editor. Please treat them as a study aid, and report corrections.
